# Listening to LCM Families

Having discussed concrete voicings and placements, let's now turn to listening to Harmonic Pattern Theory.

Listening to music is a subjective experience. 
In HPT the experience is conceived of as a pleasure/pain reaction to pattern recognition.
This ties into consonance; a complex subject, depending both on harmonicity, timbre and culture - 
[see related research on consonance.](/docs/related-research/consonance)

Lacking the means for a statistical study i will instead write of my own experience of listening to HPT related music.
General statements of how things sound are thus to be understood as my experience of how they sound.

We start with static harmony, where we let a fix set of frequencies play. Then we move on to dynamic harmony, where we change frequencies along with a rhythm.

Several of these are traditional harmonic-sounding chords and scales (Major 13 is the major scale). 

LCM 15 and 20 map to no traditional chords and sound a bit tense.

LCM 24 also sounds a bit tense. Technically it maps to the chord major 13, but that is more of a scale than a chord -
where chords are meant to function as static blocks, scales are rather sets of keys from which to draw melody and chords.

Perhaps tension from the high lcms is a feature of the high number of notes, although not uniquely as lcm 18 and 20 both have 6 notes but 18 is decidedly more pleasant than 20.

## Dynamic Harmony
For dynamic harmony we need chord accompaniment, rhythm and melody.

Rhythm is discussed in the [related research on rhythm.](/docs/related-research/rhythm)

For chords and melody we have several different options.
The most naive approach is perhaps to simply select an lcm, draw a few notes from it as a static chord, then draw notes from the same lcm to play some melody.

### LCM-based Scales and Chords

Random triads and tertians sound quite pleasant with lcms 1 to 12 and 18, a bit less so with lcm 24 and least plasant with lcm 20 and 15.

This mixed outcome of randomly drawing melody and chords from lcms sets is quite interesting.
HPT is not directly a perfect fit to music, since then all lcms would be pleasant, but it does fit somewhat since all the lcms 12 and lower sound quite nice.

Let's look at some traditional non-lcm scales and chords for comparison, and maybe glean some insight into what is going on.

### Traditional Scales and Chords
Let's look at the harmonic minor, the major blues and the pentatonic scale.

The harmonic minor harm@0 = `{0, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 11}` is quite close to 15@2, having exchanged key 10 for key 8, but it sounds more pleasant than pure lcm 15.

The major pentatonic pent@0 = `{0, 2, 4, 7, 9}` is a subset of several placements, namely 15@4,11, 18@2,7 and 24@0,5,7. 
Accompanied by minor and major triads it sounds quite pleasant.

The major blues blues@0 = `{0, 2, 3, 4, 7, 9}` is the major pentatonic but with an added key 3. 
This makes it adjacent to 8@0, 15@2,6,11, 20@0 18@5, and 24@3,5.
Accompanied by minor and major triads it sounds quite pleasant, especially with a faster beat, increased syncopation and lowered resolution to make it "swing".
